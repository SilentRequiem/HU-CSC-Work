Terrorism: Intentional violence or threat of violence by sub-national actors against noncombatants, used to intimidate or coerce in pursuit of political, religious, economic, or social goals.
National Security: A nation's ability to defend itself and protect its population, infrastructure, and sovereignty.
Terror ≠ Terrorism: Not all acts that cause fear are terrorism; terrorism requires political motivation and specific criteria.
Sub-national Actor: A group or individual not officially representing a recognized state (e.g., terrorist organizations).
U.S. Department of State Definition of Terrorism: Premeditated, politically motivated violence against noncombatant targets by sub-national groups or clandestine agents.
Hoffman Definition (2009): Violence or the threat of violence used in pursuit of a political aim.
Global Terrorism Database (GTD) Definition: An act that is intentional, violent (or threatened), committed by a sub-national actor, plus at least two of the following: political goal, intent to intimidate/coerce, or violation of international humanitarian law.
Anti-abortion Terrorism: Violence targeting abortion providers to intimidate or stop abortions.
Racial Terrorism: Violence motivated by racial supremacy (e.g., KKK).
Religious Terrorism: Violence justified through religious ideology (e.g., jihadist terrorism such as 9/11).
Eco-terrorism: Violence or sabotage framed as protecting the environment (e.g., attacks on oil infrastructure).
Is terrorism increasing or decreasing?: Currently increasing, especially domestic terrorism, influenced by political climate.
Who commits terrorism?: Increasingly domestic actors rather than foreign organizations.
Common targets of terrorism: Individuals, groups, symbolic buildings, and critical infrastructure.
Effectiveness of terrorism: Can influence or force political change, but not always successfully.
Terrorism as a Rare Event: Occurs infrequently, making it statistically difficult to study and compare.
Vested Interests: Political, financial, professional, or strategic motivations can bias terrorism research.
Misattributed Attacks: Attacks wrongly claimed, speculated on, or falsely assigned.
Fast-Food Research Problem: Quick, low-quality research dominates terrorism studies and weakens conclusions.
Plausible: Logically reasonable.
Testable (Falsifiable): Can be empirically tested.
Parsimonious: Simple but explanatory.
Actionable: Useful for real-world policy decisions.
Positivism: Focuses on observable factors; assumes behavior is shaped by physical, mental, or social forces rather than free will.
Cesare Lombroso - Atavism: Claimed criminals are biologically less evolved (now considered false and pseudoscientific).
Social Disorganization Theory (Shaw & McKay, 1938): Crime and terrorism emerge from weakened community controls in disorganized neighborhoods.
Key Conditions of Social Disorganization: Population heterogeneity, residential instability, and concentrated disadvantage.
Anomie (Merton, 1938): Breakdown of social norms leading to alienation and deviance.
Strain Theory (Agnew, 1992): Inability to achieve valued goals creates stress that may lead to crime or violence.
General Strain Theory (GST): Stress and frustration can increase the likelihood of violence, but most stressed people do not commit terrorism.
Agnew's Collective Strains (2010): Terrorism more likely when strain is high, affects civilians, is perceived as unjust, and is inflicted by powerful others.
Critique of Strain Theory: Empirical evidence is weak; strain alone does not explain terrorism well.
LaFree & Bersani (2014): Found geographic patterns consistent with social disorganization, but insufficient to prove causation.
Opportunity vs Social Disorganization Debate: Terrorist targets may be chosen due to weak control OR because they are symbolic and attractive.
Control Theory: Most people don't commit crime because of social bonds and constraints.
ACIB (Hirschi): Attachment, Commitment, Involvement, Beliefs prevent crime and terrorism.
Poverty Causes Terrorism: False — many terrorists are not poor.
Mental Illness Causes Terrorism: False — no consistent psychological profile of terrorists.
Depression Explains Terrorism: False — not supported by evidence.
